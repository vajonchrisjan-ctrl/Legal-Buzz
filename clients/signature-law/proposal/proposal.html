Who we are

Law firms only — and the click is only the beginning.

We work with law firms, and we measure the things that end in an enquiry, not the things that look good in a dashboard.

How we work

  • Evidence and limitations stay separate. Every figure in this document carries its source, its exact setting and the time it was collected.
  • We reject misleading metrics. Where the only number available would have overstated your position, we left the space empty and said why.
  • We show our corrections. When a later round contradicts an earlier one, the correction is printed, not quietly removed.
  • AI search is a first-class surface. What an assistant can read about you now decides whether it can recommend you later.
  • A failed check is not a zero. "We could not measure it" and "it is nothing" are different sentences, and we never swap one for the other.

What we measured

  • All 46 indexable URLs on your site, one request per second, in served HTML and again after full browser rendering
  • Lighthouse 12.2.1, mobile and desktop, on your homepage and your conveyancing page
  • Your entry on the Australian Business Register, including every registered business name
  • Eight host canonicalisation variants, your security and cache headers, and your image delivery
  • Three directory listings read in full — Localsearch, LawTap and LinkedIn

What we did not do

We changed nothing. No listing was claimed or edited, no form was submitted, no account was created, no tracker project was set up, and nothing was purchased. This round was entirely read-only, and every finding below describes your site exactly as a visitor met it on 27 October 2026.
We did not measure rankings, search volumes, cross-domain traffic share, AI-platform visibility or backlinks. Those need tracking tools that were not connected for this round. Rather than substitute a weaker number that looks like the real one, we have shown each as Not measured with its reason, and priced the work to establish them separately.

04
How AI has changed search

An assistant can only recommend what it can read.

We could not test what the assistants say about you. We could test what they would find if they looked — and that part is measurable.

What we did not test, and why

We did not run discovery or branded questions on ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, Google AI Overview or AI Mode.

Doing it properly requires a neutral research account on each platform, run in a fresh session, with the question set held identical across platforms. Running them from a personal signed-in account returns a personalised answer, which is not a measurement of anything. No account of that kind was available for this round, so no mention rate, no citation share and no "AI visibility score" appears anywhere in this document — not even a rough one. When we do run it, the result will be reported as a sample mention rate per platform, never as market share, and scoped to the platforms we actually tested.

The pattern this produces

A model asked "who is Signature Law?" has enough to answer. A model asked "who should I use for conveyancing in Bowral?" has almost nothing to match you on.

That gap between branded recall and discovery recall is the normal failure mode for a firm with good people and unmarked-up pages — and it is the part that closes fastest, because it is markup and description rather than reputation.

Traffic distribution — and why this page has no chart on it

Compatible keyword-level cross-domain traffic data unavailable.

A proposal of this kind normally carries a chart titled "Estimated organic traffic distribution within covered results", showing how the traffic available on your service phrases divides between you, other firms, government and directory sites. It requires one specific metric — estimated organic traffic for a single keyword at a single domain — from a tool that was not connected for this round.

We could have filled this page. Domain-level traffic estimates are easy to obtain and would have produced a confident-looking chart. They answer a different question — all traffic a domain gets from every keyword it ranks for — and presenting one in place of the other would have misstated your position in either direction. So the item stands as not measured, with the reason, and no substitute number appears.

Your SEO and AI action plan

One decision first, then nineteen fixes in a deliberate order.

The decision is yours and it changes the shape of everything after it. The fixes do not change, only their scope.

The decision: how many places, and how many brands?

Option A
We recommend

One brand, one town

Signature Law, Bowral. Everything — markup, listings, content — points at one address and one name. Fastest to a result, cheapest to run, and the strongest single local signal.

It depends on one thing: that Moss Vale is not a trading office. If it is, this option leaves a real office invisible and Option B becomes correct instead.
Option B

One brand, two towns

Signature Law with genuine Bowral and Moss Vale presences — a real page per office, separate markup, and a listing for each.

Fair to it: if you do trade from Moss Vale, this is simply the honest description of your business, and several competitors already market that town. It costs more, takes longer to mature, and demands that the second office have real staffed substance — a page for an address you rarely attend damages trust rather than building it.
Option C

Two brands

Signature Law for legal work, The HR Brief developed as a separate employment/HR-facing property.

Fair to it: your published work is dominated by employment and industrial relations, so there is real substance behind the second name, and it would reach an audience the law-firm brand does not. But it splits a small firm's attention and authority across two properties, and we would not start here. It is a decision to revisit once the first one is working — not a reason to delay this.
Our recommendation is Option A, and it is conditional. We are recommending it on the explicit assumption that Bowral is your only trading office. That assumption has not been confirmed — your own LinkedIn page contradicts it. If you tell us Moss Vale trades, our recommendation changes to Option B on the spot, and we would rather change it now than defend it later.

Why this order

Measurement before everything
Items 1 and 7 come first because without them nothing that follows can be shown to have worked. Fix the site first and measure second, and every gain becomes invisible — a baseline taken late cannot be recovered retrospectively.
Then the cheap, certain wins
Items 3 to 6 are low-effort, site-wide and need nothing from you beyond the architecture choice. None of them should wait for content, budget or a competitor analysis.
Speed where it converts
Item 8 sits above content work because your conveyancing page — the one most likely to be reached by someone ready to instruct — is your slowest on mobile, at 6.1 seconds.
Trust and content last, deliberately
Items 11 to 18 need your time and judgement. They are valuable and easy to stall on, so they follow the fixes that need nothing from you. Nothing in items 1 to 10 is blocked by them.

How the traffic metric was handled

The required metric is estimated organic traffic for one keyword at one domain. Before using any such figure we apply an arithmetic acceptance test: sum the traffic column across every returned row and compare it to the domain's reported organic traffic. If the rows sum to approximately the total and no single row equals or exceeds it, the metric is row-level and we accept it. Otherwise we reject it.

Metrics we reject on sight, because each answers a different question: per-URL "total traffic" in a SERP overview (it counts every keyword that URL ranks for); domain-level traffic in a competitive-research overview; and any vendor's domain-level traffic estimate.

For this round the test was never reached, because no tool offering the metric was connected. The finding stands as printed on page 9: Compatible keyword-level cross-domain traffic data unavailable. No domain-level figure has been substituted anywhere in this document.

This is round one, so there is nothing yet to correct. We print this section empty rather than omit it, because it will not stay empty. Audits of this kind routinely revise their own first draft: a listing wrongly called a duplicate, a "no listing found" that a fourth search disproves, a stale-address claim the live page does not support, a links-versus-domains count stated in the wrong unit.
